# WSET Level 3 progress audit — framework

**Purpose:** a periodic, honest check on whether learning is actually
in line with the WSET Level 3 Award in Wines specification (2022
Issue 2) and with `education/CURRICULUM.md`. This audit is a
study-planning check. It is **not** an official WSET score, grade,
or prediction, and it must never be presented as one.

**Who can run it:** any tutor — Muse, ChatGPT voice mode, or another
tutor — using only evidence in `SESSION_LOG.md` and `PROGRESS.md`,
plus the lesson plans in `lessons/`. Do not use chat memory, personal
tasting notes, or familiarity as evidence. If it is not logged with
a date and an actual answer, it is not evidence.

**When to run it:**

- After every chapter checkpoint, before marking anything
  ready-to-move-on.
- At regular intervals even without a checkpoint: at least once
  after every three to four study sessions, and once before
  enrolling in a formal Level 3 course.
- Whenever a spaced review is due (approximately 1 week and
  3 weeks after a checkpoint), as the review itself.
- Whenever the learner says "I think I know this now" — that is
  exactly when an audit is most useful, because familiarity is
  not understanding.

## What the audit checks — seven checks, in order

### 1. Coverage vs the specification and CURRICULUM.md

For each chapter, list every essential topic in `CURRICULUM.md`
and in the lesson plan, and mark it:

- **Introduced:** the topic was explained, but the learner has not
  yet demonstrated understanding.
- **Covered:** the topic was discussed, but recall or application
  still needs checking.
- **Practised:** the learner attempted an explanation or exercise.
- **Functionally understood:** the learner explained it accurately,
  largely unaided, in a causal form, and ideally in a new case.
- **Needs review:** a known gap, misconception, or uncertainty.
- **Missing:** the topic has not been addressed at all in logged
  evidence. Do not confuse "not recorded" with "unknown" — but do
  not count it as covered either.

Prior conversation is **exposure**, not coverage, unless it is
logged as a lesson with an actual answer. A topic that was only
mentioned in passing is introduced, at best.

### 2. Alignment — Level 3 depth, or drift?

For each chapter, ask: is the teaching actually at Level 3 depth?

- **Too shallow (Level 2 drift):** recalling names, grapes, and
  regions without explaining why — climate, production, and
  composition leading to style, quality, and price.
- **At Level 3:** causal explanations, comparisons, and application
  to unfamiliar cases, with correct terminology used precisely.
- **Too advanced (detail drift):** deep producer, soil chemistry,
  or winemaking detail that does not serve a WSET learning outcome,
  at the expense of essential topics. Detail is not depth if it
  crowds out the syllabus.

Flag drift in either direction, with an example. Do not praise
detail for its own sake, and do not dismiss a learner's interest
in detail — note it as "interesting, not examinable" where relevant,
and return to the syllabus.

### 3. Causal understanding vs memorisation

Count, from logged answers, how many explanations were causal
(condition → mechanism → grape composition or production →
style, quality, or price) versus how many were lists, names, or
one-line recitations. A chapter that passes recall but fails
causal explanation has **not** passed its checkpoint, because
the checkpoint requires two independent causal explanations.
Guided answers do not count as unaided causal understanding.
If a learner can name a region but cannot explain why its wine
tastes as it does, that is memorisation, not Level 3 understanding.

### 4. Tasting (SAT) progress, in parallel

Check SAT separately from theory. Theory progress does not imply
SAT progress.

- Has a structured white note been written in SAT order?
- Has a structured red note been written in SAT order?
- Has a blind note been written, with a quality and readiness
  conclusion that follows from the description?
- Are personal tasting notes being mistaken for SAT evidence?
  Personal notes are exposure unless written in SAT form and
  logged as such.

As of the Nov 3 state, **no formally assessed SAT blind-tasting
note is recorded**. An audit must say that plainly, not fill the
gap with tasting experience.

### 5. Gaps from the Nov 3 session — still open until evidenced otherwise

These gaps were explicitly logged on Nov 3 and must be checked
in every audit until they are closed by logged, unaided evidence:

- Growth-cycle vocabulary as a systematic set (dormancy, budburst,
  flowering, fruit set, véraison, ripening, harvest, leaf fall).
- Root-zone oxygen, distinguished from berry rot: waterlogging
  suffocates roots, even without rot.
- Mild versus severe water stress, and timing (before vs after
  véraison), and berry size and skin-to-juice effects.
- Frost/topography nuances: cold-air drainage, frost pockets,
  mid-slope safety, and the correction that frost risk is not
  universally higher on elevated slopes.
- Climate-type distinctions: maritime vs continental vs
  Mediterranean, by mechanism and by risk (disease, frost/
  vintage variation, drought/heat), independently checked,
  not just introduced.
- Geography corrections from Nov 3: Los Carneros spans Napa and
  Sonoma; Anderson Valley vs Napa latitude comparisons must be
  framed correctly; morning fog burning off is not itself
  diurnal range.
- MLF vs lees ageing, and oak effects, as a review item from
  PROGRESS.md: malic-to-lactic conversion, sensory and acidity
  impact, and distinction from lees.
- Mosel Kabinett and sweetness (Prädikat vs residual sugar),
  Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc, Gewurztraminer, and Chablis,
  as prior diagnostic uncertainties.
- Tasting vocabulary: precision and delicacy, and tannin
  terminology (intensity vs quality, firmness vs texture).

An audit that does not mention these gaps has not read
SESSION_LOG.md and PROGRESS.md, and should be rerun.

### 6. Spaced review — due dates

For every chapter that has passed a checkpoint (none yet, as of
Nov 3), the audit must list:

- Checkpoint date.
- Review due at approximately 1 week after the checkpoint.
- Review due at approximately 3 weeks after the checkpoint.
- Whether each review was done, what new example was used,
  and whether it passed or reopened review.

A later failed recall reopens review; it does not erase earlier
work, and it does not retroactively cancel a checkpoint — but it
does mean the chapter is not currently "ready" in practice, and
the audit must say so. For chapters that have not passed a
checkpoint, spaced review does not apply yet; instead, list the
next teaching step and the gap it addresses.

### 7. What NOT to claim

Every audit report must end with an explicit "Not claimed" section:

- No chapter is mastered unless it meets the CURRICULUM.md
  definition: repeated successful application in unfamiliar
  examples and delayed recall.
- No chapter is ready-to-move-on unless it meets all five
  checkpoint elements, with logged evidence.
- No SAT progress is claimed unless a SAT-form note is logged.
- No WSET exam readiness is predicted. A study audit is not
  an exam score.

If an audit cannot find evidence for a claim, it must say
"no evidence found", not "probably known".

## How to run an audit — steps

1. Read fresh copies of `STATE.md`, `CURRICULUM.md`, `PROGRESS.md`,
   and `SESSION_LOG.md`. Do not rely on a previous audit's summary.
2. For each chapter, fill in the coverage table in
   `education/audit-template.md` from logged evidence only,
   citing dates and actual answers or scores.
3. Run the seven checks above, in order, and write findings as
   evidence, not impressions. Quote or faithfully summarise actual
   learner answers; do not invent them.
4. Produce a gap list with priority: Priority 1 = blocks the next
   chapter or the course enrolment; Priority 2 = needed for a
   checkpoint; Priority 3 = useful depth, not blocking.
5. State what to revisit next, in order, and what the next lesson
   should be. Do not list everything at once as "to review".
6. Update `STATE.md` last, only if the audit changes the resume
   point or a status, and log the audit itself in `SESSION_LOG.md`
   as an audit, not as a lesson. Do not rewrite historical session
   evidence. If you are in ChatGPT voice mode, you cannot push to
   GitHub — use the manual log template in `VOICE-MODE-GUIDE.md`
   instead, and say so plainly.

## Audit frequency and the course timeline

- Goal: WSET Level 3 course in early 2027, exam by April 2027.
- An audit before enrolment is essential: it should confirm
  whether Chapter 1 and the foundations are actually ready, and
  which regions are still "not recorded" in PROGRESS.md, so the
  course is not enrolled in on the basis of familiarity alone.
- After enrolment, audits should follow the course's own
  assessments, not replace them. This framework is for self-study
  before and alongside a course, not a substitute for it.
