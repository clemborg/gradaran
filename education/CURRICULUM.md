# WSET Level 3 preparation — chapter curriculum

**Created:** 2026-11-01. This is our own staged self-study sequence, **aligned with** the [official WSET Level 3 Award in Wines qualification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/qualifications/wset-level-3-award-in-wines) and its 2022 Issue 2 specification, not a claim to reproduce the WSET textbook's chapter numbering. Course enrollment goal: **the learner's own goal** — set it in their own copy.

## What a chapter status means

| Status | Definition |
|---|---|
| Not started | The chapter has not been systematically taught in this track. |
| In progress | At least one lesson completed; some objectives still pending. |
| Ready to move on | Broad and functional foundation shown at the **chapter checkpoint**; return for spaced review. **Not WSET exam-ready or mastered.** |
| Review due | A previously completed chapter needs spaced recall or a known gap addressed. |
| Mastered | Only after repeated successful application in unfamiliar examples and delayed recall; not needed to progress through the first pass. |

**Ready-to-move-on checkpoint for every chapter:**
1. Address every essential topic in that chapter at least once. Prior conversation counts as *exposure*, not an exemption from the checkpoint.
2. Achieve at least **80%** on a short (about 8–10 questions) unaided recall check, with corrections for errors.
3. Give **two accurate, largely independent “why/how” explanations** linking **growing environment or production decisions → grape composition → wine style, quality or price** (or equivalent chapter-specific causal links). Answering the question with guidance is not yet a pass.
4. Handle **one new comparison or application scenario** without a material conceptual mistake.
5. Log the actual completion date, score, evidence and outstanding uncertainties in [PROGRESS.md](PROGRESS.md) / [SESSION_LOG.md](SESSION_LOG.md). Schedule a later review (approximately 1 week and 3 weeks). A later failed recall reopens review, not erases earlier work.

The checkpoint is a **study-planning heuristic**, not an official WSET requirement or predicted examination score. If an important topic has not been explained well, keep the chapter In progress even if a numerical threshold was met.

## Chapter 1 — Natural factors in the vineyard

**Why first:** Later region-specific questions depend on understanding *why* grapes ripen differently in different places. This maps to WSET Unit 1 Learning Outcome 1, natural vineyard factors.

**Lesson 1.1 — The vine and annual cycle:** vine structures (green growth, one-year-old wood, permanent wood), dormancy, budburst, flowering, fruit set, véraison, harvest/ripening.

**Lesson 1.2 — The grape and vine needs:** water, warmth, sunlight, nutrients; grape sugars, malic/tartaric acids, colour, tannin and flavour compounds; how ripeness relates to potential alcohol, acidity, tannin maturity and flavour.

**Lesson 1.3 — Regional climates:** cool/moderate/warm/hot; continental/maritime/Mediterranean; latitude, altitude, oceans/lakes, diurnal range.

**Lesson 1.4 — Site and vintage:** aspect, slope, fog, soil water availability and drainage, weather versus climate, vintage variability.

**Lesson 1.5 — Integration and checkpoint:** compare two unfamiliar vineyards; explain the expected differences in ripening and eventual wine characteristics. Use eight to ten recall questions, two causal explanations and one new site comparison under the common chapter checkpoint.

**Teaching examples:** California maritime winds and fog, Ukiah vs Anderson Valley, regional temperature differences, slope/drainage, acidity and ripeness are useful examples for this chapter. They are teaching examples, **not a completion date** — the chapter is not started until the first lesson is taught and logged in the learner's own copy.

**Initial teaching question (ask one at a time):** *During a grapevine's annual cycle, what happens at budburst, flowering, fruit set and véraison?*
