# Key regions at a glance — comparison sketches only

Spoken summary: These sketches are for comparison, not for learning
a region in full. Each region gets one spoken paragraph: climate,
grapes, and the logic that links them. For the full lesson, use the
chapter file in `lessons/`. Burgundy is cool, semi-continental,
Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, classified by site. Bordeaux is maritime,
blended, Cabernet on gravel on the Left Bank and Merlot on clay on
the Right Bank, with Sauternes sweet wines from noble rot. The Loire
runs from maritime Muscadet in the west to continental Sancerre and
Chenin in the centre and east. Alsace is continental, dry, and sunny
behind the Vosges, labelled by grape. The Rhône runs from cool,
steep Syrah in the north to warm, blended Grenache in the south.
Germany is cool, Riesling-led, labelled by ripeness. Italy runs from
Nebbiolo in Piedmont to Sangiovese in Tuscany to Etna's altitude
wines in Sicily. Spain runs from Rioja's ageing law to Ribera's
altitude to Priorat's slate. Portugal runs from Atlantic Vinho Verde
to the Douro's terraces. California runs from coastal fog to inland
heat, with Carneros cool and Calistoga warm in the same valley.
Chile runs from coastal Casablanca to inland Maipo, and Argentina
from Mendoza's altitude to Salta's high Torrontés. South Africa runs
from coastal Walker Bay to inland Stellenbosch to Swartland old vines.
Australia runs from Barossa warmth to Tasmanian cool, and New Zealand
from Marlborough's pungent Sauvignon Blanc to Central Otago's
continental Pinot Noir. Champagne, sparkling, and fortified wines are
method chapters, not place chapters, and they are sketched at the end.

## France

Burgundy: cool, semi-continental, limestone slopes, Pinot Noir and
Chardonnay, village to grand cru by site, producer matters as much
as classification. Chablis: coolest Burgundy, Kimmeridgian limestone,
lean, high-acid Chardonnay, often unoaked. Beaujolais: Gamay, granite
crus in the north, carbonic maceration for Nouveau.

Bordeaux: maritime, blended. Left Bank: gravel, Cabernet-led,
structured, ageworthy. Right Bank: clay and limestone, Merlot-led,
softer, earlier. Sauternes/Barsac: fog, noble rot, sweet, high-acid,
ageworthy.

Loire: maritime west to continental centre and east. Muscadet:
light, lees-aged, Atlantic. Sancerre/Pouilly-Fumé: Sauvignon Blanc,
limestone and flint, mineral and citrus. Touraine/Vouvray: Chenin
Blanc, dry to sweet, still and sparkling.

Alsace: continental, Vosges rain shadow, dry and sunny, labelled
by grape: Riesling, Gewurztraminer, Pinot Gris, Muscat, from dry
to Vendanges Tardives and Sélection de Grains Nobles sweet.

Rhône north: steep, continental, Syrah, with Viognier in Côte-Rôtie
and Condrieu. Rhône south: Mediterranean, Grenache-Syrah-Mourvèdre
blends, galets roulés in Châteauneuf-du-Pape. Provence: pale, dry
rosé by direct pressing.

## Germany, Austria, Tokaj

Germany: cool, marginal, Riesling, steep slate slopes, labelled by
ripeness (Prädikat) and by dryness (trocken). Austria: continental,
Grüner Veltliner and Riesling, Wachau, Kamptal, Burgenland.
Tokaj: Furmint, noble rot, aszú berries, sweet, high-acid, ageworthy.

## Italy

Piedmont: Nebbiolo (Barolo, Barbaresco), Barbera, Dolcetto, Moscato
d'Asti; fog, hills, long autumn. Veneto: Valpolicella, Amarone by
appassimento, Soave, Prosecco. Tuscany: Sangiovese (Chianti Classico,
Brunello), Super Tuscans, Vin Santo. Centre and south: Montepulciano,
Aglianico, Nero d'Avola. Etna: altitude, volcanic soil, Nerello
Mascalese and Carricante, cool and elegant within Sicily.

## Spain and Portugal

Spain: Rioja (Tempranillo, ageing law, American oak), Ribera del
Duero (altitude, Tinto Fino), Priorat (licorella slate, old Garnacha
and Cariñena), Rías Baixas (Albariño, Atlantic). Portugal: Vinho
Verde (Atlantic, light, high-acid), Douro (terraced, Touriga Nacional
and field blends), Dão, Alentejo.

## USA and Canada

California: Pacific cooling, fog, Coast Ranges, valley orientation.
Carneros cool (Pinot, Chardonnay), Calistoga warm (Cabernet), in the
same Napa Valley. Sonoma: Russian River, Sonoma Coast, Dry Creek,
Alexander. Mendocino: Anderson Valley cool, Ukiah warm. Monterey and
Santa Barbara: ocean and east-west valleys. Paso Robles warm, Lodi
and Sierra Foothills historic Zinfandel. Oregon: Willamette Valley
Pinot Noir, cool and maritime. Washington: Columbia Valley, continental,
dry, irrigated, Cabernet, Merlot, Syrah. Canada: Niagara, Okanagan,
icewine from grapes frozen on the vine.

## Chile, Argentina, South Africa

Chile: Pacific valleys east to west, coastal Casablanca and Leyda
cool (Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay), inland Maipo and Colchagua warm
(Cabernet, Carménère). Argentina: Mendoza altitude (Malbec),
Salta high Torrontés, Patagonia cool (Pinot, sparkling).
South Africa: Western Cape, coastal Walker Bay and Elgin cool,
Stellenbosch and Paarl warm, Swartland old vines, Chenin Blanc
(Steen) and Pinotage.

## Australia and New Zealand

Australia: Barossa warm Shiraz, McLaren Vale warm, Margaret River
maritime Cabernet, Hunter Semillon (light, ageworthy), Clare and
Eden Riesling, Yarra and Tasmania cool Pinot and Chardonnay.
New Zealand: Marlborough pungent Sauvignon Blanc, Central Otago
continental Pinot Noir, Hawke's Bay Syrah and Chardonnay,
Martinborough Pinot Noir.

## Sparkling and fortified — method sketches

Sparkling: traditional method (bottle fermentation, lees ageing,
autolysis) for Champagne, Crémant, Cava, Franciacorta; tank method
for Prosecco; transfer as a middle path; carbonation as the simplest.
Champagne: Chardonnay, Pinot Noir, Meunier; chalk; non-vintage vs
vintage; dosage from Brut Nature to Doux.

Fortified: Port fortified during fermentation, therefore sweet,
from the Douro, as Ruby, Tawny, Vintage, and LBV. Sherry fortified
after fermentation, therefore mostly dry, from Jerez, as Fino
(under flor) to Oloroso (oxidative), with a solera and no vintage.
Madeira heated and oxidised, from dry Sercial to sweet Malmsey,
virtually indestructible after opening.
