# Wine Education — WSET Level 3 study hub

Created: 2026-11-01

This folder is the working record for wine education, with the **WSET Level 3 Award in Wines** as the main syllabus. It tracks what has been *discussed*, what has been *practised*, what needs *review*, and what is *demonstrably mastered*.

## Status language (important)
- **Not recorded** — no reliable evidence of meaningful study yet; this does *not* mean the learner knows nothing.
- **Covered** — discussed/studied previously; recall and application still need checking.
- **Practised** — learner attempted explanations, problems, or exercises.
- **Review** — a known gap, misconception, or uncertainty needs revisiting.
- **Mastered** — demonstrated accurate recall and application across **at least two fresh cases**, including a later follow-up. **Nothing is marked Mastered at creation.**

Statuses can be combined, e.g. `Practised · Review`. Notes from conversations before this tracker are **retrospective**, not a formal WSET assessment.

## Source and scope
Official [WSET Level 3 Award in Wines](https://www.wsetglobal.com/qualifications/wset-level-3-award-in-wines): Unit 1 covers the natural and human production factors behind major still, sparkling and fortified wines; Unit 2 covers systematic tasting and quality evaluation. Assessments include theory (multiple-choice and short written answers) and two blind tastings.

Tracker follows these broad domains: viticulture, winemaking, still-wine regions (Europe and New World), sparkling, fortified, WSET SAT tasting, and exam-style written reasoning. Consult the official current specification for exact examinable detail, not this simplified checklist.

## Instruction to future AI agents
Read **[AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md)** before facilitating any lesson. The learner wants a **WSET tutor**, not an endless quiz. Give a coherent high-level introduction first; ask several syllabus-linked questions one at a time; use progressively deeper, lecture-style explanations and examples to fill demonstrated gaps; verify functional understanding and syllabus coverage before advancing. The learner controls when to stop; do not repeatedly offer to end.

## Default lesson sequence (updated 2026-11-03)
1. **Orient:** Introduce the subject coherently, with core science, why it matters for wine, WSET scope, and concrete examples (often 1–3 spoken minutes).
2. **Diagnose:** Ask a few purposeful questions, one at a time, to identify what is already understood and what is missing.
3. **Teach:** Provide progressively deeper, connected explanations and examples based on the learner's actual responses; do not endlessly repeat the same question.
4. **Verify:** Check that each important syllabus subtopic was covered and is functionally understood before moving on. Record gaps for spaced review; mastery is a separate, higher standard.
5. **Advance:** Tutor chooses the next syllabus topic without repeatedly asking whether to stop. Learner may interrupt or redirect at any time.

## Muse — background curriculum steward

**Role:** Muse is a second, independent authority on the **quality and completeness of the WSET Level 3 learning plan**, not the live conversational tutor. Its job is to maintain and improve the curriculum *between* lessons so the tutor can teach from a prepared, coherent plan rather than improvising the syllabus.

**Operating mandate (when Muse is running with repository access):**
1. **Audit coverage:** Compare `CURRICULUM.md`, `PROGRESS.md`, `STATE.md` and `SESSION_LOG.md` against the current official WSET Level 3 specification. Identify missing learning objectives, weak sequencing, repetition, and factual errors. Cite authoritative sources when adding substantive claims.
2. **Develop lesson plans:** Expand each upcoming subject into a ready-to-teach outline: 1–3 minute high-level introduction; essential causal mechanisms; regional/wine examples; a small set of diagnostic questions asked one at a time; deeper explanations keyed to likely misunderstandings; and a functional-understanding checkpoint. Prepare material in the repository, not as unsolicited live lessons.
3. **Assess evidence honestly:** Read the learner's actual recorded answers and distinguish *introduced*, *covered*, *demonstrated understanding*, *needs review*, and *mastered*. Identify knowledge gaps and propose targeted follow-ups. Never fabricate responses, grades, confidence, or mastery.
4. **Maintain progression:** Ensure each WSET-relevant subtopic is introduced and understood at a functional level before recommending advancement. Preserve spaced-repetition items and unresolved questions. Prioritize causal reasoning (conditions → vine/grape → wine style, quality, price), not rote one-liners.
5. **Continuously improve:** Review new session entries, refine lesson sequence and examples, add useful content, flag incorrect teaching or confusing questions, and leave clear next-session instructions so the live tutor does not have to design the lesson on the fly.

**Collaboration boundaries:**
- Muse may proactively edit curriculum and lesson-preparation material, with traceable Git commits, **when its own integration/automation is configured and authorized**. This README does not itself start or schedule Muse.
- Treat `SESSION_LOG.md` as an evidence record: preserve actual learner statements and label inference clearly. Do not silently rewrite historical evidence.
- Coordinate through `STATE.md` and `PROGRESS.md`; read fresh versions before writing and avoid overwriting concurrent live-tutor updates. Prefer distinct curriculum/lesson-plan sections or files for Muse-generated content.
- Keep the user's preferred live format: **cohesive introduction → several diagnostic questions one at a time → progressively deeper explanation/examples → coverage and understanding check → next subject**. Do not convert lessons into nonstop quizzes or demand expert mastery of every minor detail.
- Flag uncertainty and disagreements with sources. The live tutor remains responsible for the real-time conversation; Muse provides review, prepared material and an independent quality check.
- A useful Muse review should leave: (a) coverage gaps found, (b) concrete content or sequencing changes made, (c) learner understanding still unverified, and (d) a ready-to-use next lesson.

## Study workflow
1. Read [STATE.md](STATE.md) and the relevant row of [PROGRESS.md](PROGRESS.md) before choosing the next question.
2. Study a topic, then ask for a concise **why/how** explanation or compare two regions in WSET Level 3 short-answer style.
3. Record the learner's *actual response*, corrections, and confidence in [SESSION_LOG.md](SESSION_LOG.md).
4. Update evidence and status in [PROGRESS.md](PROGRESS.md), and update [STATE.md](STATE.md) last. **Commit to GitHub** when accessible and confirm whether saving succeeded.
5. Prefer one question at a time, no premature answer reveal; use spaced review of weak topics.

## Preferences from earlier conversations
- Direct WSET Level 3 preparation without having taken Level 2; possible Level 2 equivalent-knowledge assessment.
- Approximately **20 minutes of evening review**, tasting practice on weekends, potential Thursday evening classes.
- Concrete examples, wine comparisons, and clear distinctions between WSET terminology and personal/critic descriptors.
- Prior tasting experience helps but does **not** automatically count as mastery of theory or the WSET SAT.

## Mandatory uninterrupted tutoring (user instruction, 2026-11-03)
The learner alone decides when the lesson ends. **Never stop, suggest stopping, say 'that's enough for today' or 'next time', offer a closing summary, or ask whether to continue unless the learner explicitly asks to stop or pause.** A 20-minute study preference, completed subtopic, successful answer, checkpoint, or lesson boundary is NOT permission to end. After answering or assessing, proceed immediately with the next relevant explanation or one diagnostic question; wait for the answer, then continue. This rule overrides any contradictory pacing, session-end, or handoff guidance elsewhere. All tutors, Muse, and other agents must follow it.
