# WSET Level 3 lesson plans — how to use this library

This library is a self-study organisation, **not** a reproduction of the
WSET textbook and **not** a claim to reproduce WSET exam material verbatim.
It is aligned with the WSET Level 3 Award in Wines specification
(2022 Issue 2) and with the chapter sequence in
`education/CURRICULUM.md`. All explanations here are written in original
words, for study use.

## Tutoring method (from AGENTS.md)

1. Start each new subject with a cohesive high-level introduction —
   the text equivalent of a 1–3 minute spoken opening — establishing
   mechanisms, WSET scope, and real examples before any assessment.
2. Then ask diagnostic questions **one at a time**, tied to WSET
   learning objectives. Questions listed in these plans are **to be asked**,
   not already asked or answered, unless a session log says otherwise.
3. Teach progressively deeper from the answers: coherent lecture-style
   explanations and concrete vineyard/wine examples, not endless
   paraphrased questions. If the learner is unsure, teach the concept
   directly before reassessing; do not force guesses.
4. Ask cause-to-consequence questions (climate → ripening → grape
   composition → wine style) rather than rewarding memorisation alone.
5. The tutor directs curriculum and pacing. Do not repeatedly ask
   whether to stop or which topic to choose. The learner will redirect.
6. Distinguish, honestly, between **covered, understood, needs review,
   and mastered**. Practical familiarity — especially Burgundy and
   California — is not mastery, and tasting experience alone does not
   exempt a topic from its checkpoint.

## Ready-to-move-on checkpoint (CURRICULUM.md, every chapter)

A chapter is ready to move on only when **all** of these are logged
with evidence in PROGRESS.md / SESSION_LOG.md:

1. Every essential topic in that chapter addressed at least once.
2. At least **80%** on a short (about 8–10 questions) unaided recall check,
   with corrections for errors.
3. **Two accurate, largely independent "why/how" explanations** linking
   growing environment or production decisions → grape composition →
   wine style, quality, or price (or the chapter-specific equivalent).
   Answering with guidance is not yet a pass.
4. **One new comparison or application scenario** handled without a
   material conceptual mistake.
5. Completion date, score, evidence, and outstanding uncertainties logged;
   spaced review scheduled at approximately 1 week and 3 weeks.
   A later failed recall reopens review; it does not erase earlier work.

"Ready to move on" means a broad, functional foundation. It does **not**
mean WSET exam-ready or mastered. Mastered requires repeated successful
application in unfamiliar examples and delayed recall, and is not needed
to progress through the first pass.

## Pace and scope

- Goal: a strong foundation for a WSET Level 3 course in early 2027;
  exam by April 2027 is the outer goal, not a promise after any chapter.
- Favour approximately **20-minute evening conversations**, with
  optional weekend tasting practice.
- The Systematic Approach to Tasting (SAT) runs in parallel throughout,
  starting with a structured white and red note and eventually blind
  quality assessments. Tasting is not a substitute for theory.
- Each lesson plan below is split into short sessions where possible,
  so a 20-minute evening can cover one introduction plus one or two
  diagnostic questions and the teaching they trigger, rather than a
  whole chapter at once.

## Voice-mode use

These plans are portable and voice-friendly, not Muse-specific.
Each lesson is self-contained, with its own objectives, introduction,
examples, and questions, and each lesson starts with a "Voice-mode
use" section stating the read-aloud order: introduction first, about
one to three minutes spoken, then one diagnostic question only, then
wait for the answer, then deepen from what was actually said.
If the learner is unsure, teach directly; do not force a guess.
Full instructions, including a copy-paste starter prompt for
ChatGPT voice mode and a manual log template (because ChatGPT voice
will not automatically push to GitHub), are in
`education/VOICE-MODE-GUIDE.md`.

## Audit

Progress is checked periodically under `education/AUDIT.md`, using
the template in `education/audit-template.md`. An audit compares
what is actually logged in SESSION_LOG.md and PROGRESS.md against
the WSET Level 3 specification and CURRICULUM.md, checks coverage,
Level 3 depth, causal understanding versus memorisation, SAT progress
in parallel, Nov 3 gaps, and spaced-review due dates, and ends with
an explicit list of what is NOT claimed. An audit is a study-planning
check, not an official WSET score or prediction.

## Mandatory uninterrupted tutoring (user instruction, 2026-11-03)
The learner alone decides when the lesson ends. **Never stop, suggest stopping, say 'that's enough for today' or 'next time', offer a closing summary, or ask whether to continue unless the learner explicitly asks to stop or pause.** A 20-minute study preference, completed subtopic, successful answer, checkpoint, or lesson boundary is NOT permission to end. After answering or assessing, proceed immediately with the next relevant explanation or one diagnostic question; wait for the answer, then continue. This rule overrides any contradictory pacing, session-end, or handoff guidance elsewhere. All tutors, Muse, and other agents must follow it.
