# WSET Level 3 lesson plans — how to use this library

This library is a self-study organisation, **not** a reproduction of the
WSET textbook and **not** a claim to reproduce WSET exam material verbatim.
It is aligned with the WSET Level 3 Award in Wines specification
(2022 Issue 2) and with the chapter sequence in
`education/CURRICULUM.md`. All explanations here are written in original
words, for study use.

## Status — read this first

- **No chapter checkpoint has been passed in the shared copy, and no chapter is mastered.** The shared copy contains no learner progress at all, on purpose.
- Chapters 1–17 are **plans**. A plan written does not count as covering, understanding, or passing a topic.
- Each learner starts at **Chapter 1 unless their own `STATE.md` says otherwise**. Copy this pack to your own repo first (see `education/AGENTS.md`), create your own `STATE.md`, `PROGRESS.md` and `SESSION_LOG.md` from `education/progress-templates/`, and record your own progress there.
- The governing files are `education/AGENTS.md`, `education/CURRICULUM.md`, and — in the learner's own copy — `education/STATE.md`, `education/PROGRESS.md`, and `education/SESSION_LOG.md`. If anything here conflicts with those files, the governing files win, and the conflict should be logged, not smoothed over.

## Narrative first — not rote memorisation

Teach each lesson as a connected story, not as a list of isolated facts to memorise. Each lesson should have a clear narrative thread — a vine through a season, a grape through ripening, a region through its climate and history — with facts embedded in that story, and diagnostics that test understanding of the story and its causes, not recall of a list.

- Do not quiz isolated facts (for example, "what does phosphorus do?") as standalone recall. Ask what happens next, why, and what it means for the wine.
- A fact earns its place only if it helps explain a wine style, quality or price. Cut any fact that does not.
- History and fun facts serve the story. Use at most 1–2 per session, only where they advance it — see `education/knowledge/history-fun-facts.md`. Never use a story as a substitute for the syllabus point.

## Tutoring method (from AGENTS.md)

1. Start each new subject with a cohesive high-level introduction — in voice mode, about a 1–3 minute spoken opening; in text mode, the same story in a short sequence of messages — establishing mechanisms, WSET scope, and real examples before any assessment.
2. Then ask diagnostic questions **one at a time**, tied to WSET learning objectives. Questions listed in these plans are **to be asked**, not already asked or answered, unless a session log in the learner's own copy says otherwise.
3. Teach progressively deeper from the answers: coherent explanations and concrete vineyard/wine examples, not endless paraphrased questions. If the learner is unsure, teach the concept directly before reassessing; do not force guesses.
4. Ask cause-to-consequence questions (climate → ripening → grape composition → wine style) rather than rewarding memorisation alone.
5. The tutor directs curriculum and pacing. Do not repeatedly ask whether to stop or which topic to choose. The learner will redirect.
6. Distinguish, honestly, between **covered, understood, needs review, and mastered**. Practical familiarity is not mastery, and tasting experience alone does not exempt a topic from its checkpoint.
7. **Engaging, not dry — and not fluffy.** Use vivid examples, real wines, comparisons, and the visuals in `education/visuals/` where they help. Never sacrifice accuracy for a good line, and never pad a lesson with trivia.

## Ready-to-move-on checkpoint (CURRICULUM.md, every chapter)

A chapter is ready to move on only when **all** of these are logged with evidence in the learner's own PROGRESS.md / SESSION_LOG.md:

1. Every essential topic in that chapter addressed at least once.
2. At least **80%** on a short (about 8–10 questions) unaided recall check, with corrections for errors.
3. **Two accurate, largely independent "why/how" explanations** linking growing environment or production decisions → grape composition → wine style, quality, or price (or the chapter-specific equivalent). Answering with guidance is not yet a pass.
4. **One new comparison or application scenario** handled without a material conceptual mistake.
5. Completion date, score, evidence, and outstanding uncertainties logged; spaced review scheduled at approximately 1 week and 3 weeks. A later failed recall reopens review; it does not erase earlier work.

"Ready to move on" means a broad, functional foundation. It does **not** mean WSET exam-ready or mastered. Mastered requires repeated successful application in unfamiliar examples and delayed recall, and is not needed to progress through the first pass.

## Pace and scope

- Goal: a strong foundation for a WSET Level 3 course; exam timing is the learner's own goal, not a promise after any chapter.
- Favour approximately **20-minute evening conversations**, with optional weekend tasting practice.
- The Systematic Approach to Tasting (SAT) runs in parallel throughout, starting with a structured white and red note and eventually blind quality assessments. Tasting is not a substitute for theory.
- Each lesson plan below is split into short sessions where possible, so a 20-minute evening can cover one introduction plus one or two diagnostic questions and the teaching they trigger, rather than a whole chapter at once.

## Starting point

Start at Chapter 1, natural factors in the vineyard, Lesson 1.1: the vine and annual cycle — unless the learner's own `STATE.md` says otherwise. The first diagnostic question is: *During a grapevine's annual cycle, what happens at budburst, flowering, fruit set and véraison?*

## Voice and text mode use

These plans are portable and platform-agnostic. Each lesson is self-contained, with its own objectives, introduction, examples, and questions. Each lesson starts with a "Voice-mode use" section stating the order: introduction first, about one to three minutes spoken, then one diagnostic question only, then wait for the answer, then deepen from what was actually said. In text mode, use the same order, in short messages: send the introduction in a short sequence, then one question, then wait for the reply.

Full instructions, including copy-paste starter prompts for both modes and a manual log template, are in `education/VOICE-MODE-GUIDE.md`.

## Audit

Progress is checked periodically under `education/AUDIT.md`, using the template in `education/audit-template.md`. An audit compares what is actually logged in the learner's own SESSION_LOG.md and PROGRESS.md against the WSET Level 3 specification and CURRICULUM.md, checks coverage, Level 3 depth, causal understanding versus memorisation, SAT progress in parallel, the learner's own gaps, and spaced-review due dates, and ends with an explicit list of what is NOT claimed. An audit is a study-planning check, not an official WSET score or prediction.

- `history-fun-facts.md` — history and fun facts tied to chapters; use at most 1–2 per session, only where they advance the lesson's story.

## Mandatory uninterrupted tutoring
The learner alone decides when the lesson ends. **Never stop, suggest stopping, say 'that's enough for today' or 'next time', offer a closing summary, or ask whether to continue unless the learner explicitly asks to stop or pause.** A 20-minute study preference, completed subtopic, successful answer, checkpoint, or lesson boundary is NOT permission to end. After answering or assessing, proceed immediately with the next relevant explanation or one diagnostic question; wait for the answer, then continue. This rule overrides any contradictory pacing, session-end, or handoff guidance elsewhere.
